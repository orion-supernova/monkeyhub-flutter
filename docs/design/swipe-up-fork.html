Huddles in secret rooms: yes. A huddle is live audio. Nothing is stored, so there's nothing for the secret room to protect. Vanish mode: 10 seconds after everyone has seen it

Is 10s seen-based too heavy? No, and 1 hour wouldn't be any lighter.

Load doesn't come from the duration. It comes from knowing when everyone has seen a message. Once you know that, a 10-second timer and a 1-hour timer are the same single scheduled job. So the question is only how to detect "seen by everyone" cheaply, and we already have the right primitive:

Every member has a read cursor
roomMembers.lastReadAt
→
Seen floor = the lowest cursor among the other members (someone who doesn't share counts as never)
→
Floor moves up: everything below it gets one burn job at now + 10s
  • DMs, the common case, need zero extra reads. In a DM, "everyone else" is one person, so their read cursor is the seen point for your messages. The new value is already in hand inside markRoomRead.
  • Groups read one tiny doc. vanishState (one row per vanish room, nothing for normal rooms) stores the seen floor and who the slowest reader is. A read by anyone else costs 1 read and no recompute. Only the slowest reader's read rescans the members (one indexed range read), which is the only moment the floor can move.
  • Zero cost for normal rooms. The client says whether the room is in vanish mode when it calls markRoomRead, so normal rooms never touch vanishState and the rooms row is never read (that's the OCC fix we already shipped). A client that lies can't burn anything early: the floor still comes from real cursors.
  • One burn job per floor move, not per message. It deletes the range (oldFloor, newFloor] through the room's time index in one pass. Secret rooms today schedule one expireMessage per message; vanish mode is cheaper than that.
  • Sending costs nothing extra. There's no expiresAt, no per-message scheduler, and no new index on messages.
  • The 24-hour deadline is one self-rescheduling job per room, not a cron. Details in cleanup that can't leak.
  • The countdown is free. Clients draw the 10s ring from burnAt on vanishState. Only screens open on that room subscribe to it, and the rooms row (which fans out to every member's chat list) is never patched for a burn.

Cleanup that can't leak

Two ways a vanishing message dies: early (10s after everyone has seen it) or at its 24-hour deadline. Whichever comes first wins, the other must not linger, and nothing may outlive its promise even if a job fails.

PieceHow it works
Stamped at sendEach vanishing message gets vanishUntil = sentAt + 24h. Turning vanish off later never changes messages already sent; they keep their promise.
One index for both deathsmessages.by_room_vanish ["roomId", "vanishUntil"]. Since vanishUntil moves in step with sentAt, "seen up to time T" and "deadline passed" are both just a range on this one index. Every range is bounded below by gt(0): an unset field sorts lowest, and an unbounded lt would match every normal message in the room (the incident from October).
Exactly one deadline job per roomvanishState.deadlineJobId. The job deletes everything past its deadline, then looks at the oldest survivor and schedules itself for that message's vanishUntil, cancelling any other job id it finds first. If nothing is left and vanish is off, it deletes vanishState and stops for good. So when messages die early from "seen", the deadline job finds nothing at its time and either moves on or ends; there's never a job waiting for a message that's already gone.
At most one burn job per roomvanishState holds burnAt / burnUpTo plus one queued nextUpTo. A newer "seen" while a burn is pending only updates nextUpTo; the running job finishes its range, then reschedules itself for nextUpTo 10 seconds after that point was reached. Every message still gets its full 10 seconds, and there's never more than one job.
Idempotent everywhereJobs re-read state, delete by range and tolerate "already gone". A retried or duplicated job is harmless.
Deletes the whole messageThrough the existing message-deletion path: storage blobs, reactions, mention inbox rows, and lock-screen pushes (revokeMessagePushes). Nothing is orphaned for the retention sweeps to find months later.
Never shown past its deadlineThe timeline query and the client both hide any message past vanishUntil. If the box is down for an hour, expired messages still never render; they're just deleted late.
Backstop, no new cronThe existing daily retention run gets one more bounded range (vanishUntil between 0 and now minus 1h, capped batch) for anything a lost job left behind. It's normally empty: one small read a day.
TeardownRoom deletion and account deletion plans gain vanishState (cancel both job ids, delete the row). The existing test that every table has a deletion step covers the new table.
Membership changesA joiner's vanish cursor starts at their join time, so they never hold older messages alive. A leaver stops counting immediately; if they were the slowest, the floor is recomputed in the same mutation.
Sharing turned on laterThe person's vanish cursor jumps to what they've actually read at that moment (they just agreed to share it), the floor is recomputed once, and a burn is scheduled if it moved.

Tests cover each row, including "message burned early, deadline job then finds nothing and stops" and "job killed mid-run, backstop cleans up".

Hidden "seen": 24 hours by default, faster only if everyone shares

One simple rule, and nothing ever disappears early because someone hasn't answered a question:

Every vanishing message starts on a 24-hour clock from when it's sent. It switches to "10 seconds after seen" only once everyone in the chat shares their reads. If someone starts sharing during those 24 hours, the switch happens then.

ExampleWhat happens
You and Zeynep both share "seen"She reads your message, and it vanishes 10 seconds later.
Zeynep hides "seen"Your message stays for 24 hours, read or not. You see a small "24h" clock on the bubble, never "Seen", so you can't tell whether she read it.
Zeynep starts sharing 3 hours laterAnything she has already read vanishes 10 seconds after she shares; the rest waits for her read. Nothing she hasn't read disappears.
Group of 5, Alp hides "seen"Everyone's messages there last 24 hours. Nobody can tell whether Alp hides "seen" or just hasn't read yet.

What the person who hides "seen" sees

The first time they open a vanishing chat, a card inside the chat (not a popup, and it never blocks anything) says:

DM: You don't share Seen, so Zeynep's messages to you last 24 hours. Yours still vanish 10 seconds after she sees them. Share your reads here so hers vanish too?
Group: You don't share Seen, so messages sent to you here last 24 hours. Share your reads here so they vanish 10 seconds after everyone has seen them?
You can change this anytime in Settings › Privacy.

Share my reads here Don't share here

  • Either answer posts a short note that ends with "You can change this anytime in Settings › Privacy." That line is always there.
  • Settings › Privacy › Seen: when it's set to "Nobody", a switch appears below it: "Share my reads in vanishing chats" (off by default, decided). Below that is a list of the chats where you said "Share my reads here", each removable.
  • Dismissing the card is the same as "Don't share here": messages keep the 24-hour clock.
  • Your own messages never wait for you. A message waits only for the people it was sent to, so hiding your "seen" never slows down the messages you send.
  • The switch starts off, so nothing you receive can disappear because you read it before you've chosen.

Cost: zero extra reads. The client tells markRoomRead whether this read counts. A per-chat choice is one optional field on your own roomMembers row, which markRoomRead already loads, so the server enforces it for free. A modified client can only expose its own user's reads.

Huddle notifications: the psychology

A ring summons: someone wants you, now, and ignoring it is a small rejection. A huddle should invite: join if you feel like it, and not joining is invisible. If nobody hears about it, though, huddles die empty (that's WhatsApp's "No one else joined"). So people should know, but it should feel like noticing friends at a table, not a tap on the shoulder.

Recommendation: one soft push, invitation copy, quietly withdrawn when it ends, and the starter never learns who ignored it.

What gets sent

  • Softer than a chat push. iOS interruption-level: passive (no sound, no screen wake; it waits in Notification Center). Android: a low-importance "Huddles" channel people can switch off on its own.
  • Invitation copy: "Murat is hanging out in Didimke · Drop in". In DMs: "Zeynep is free to talk". Never "is calling" or "wants to talk to you".
  • At most once per room per 2 hours, so stop-start huddles don't nag. The cooldown comes from the previous huddle's pushedAt (one indexed read), so there are no per-person cooldown rows to write.
  • Muted room: no push (the Join pill still shows in the room).
  • Groups over 20 members: in-app only, to keep fan-out and noise down. The Join pill and chat-list wave reuse the existing activeForRoom / liveInMyRooms queries, so there are no new subscriptions and no rooms patch.

What protects people

  • Withdrawn when the huddle ends. The push is removed from the lock screen through the existing dismiss path, so nobody wakes up to a dead invite and the guilt that comes with it.
  • No "seen" or "declined" for huddles. The starter never learns who got notified or who ignored it.
  • An empty huddle leaves no trace for others. "You wanted to talk · nobody joined" is a local-only bubble on the starter's device, so it costs zero writes. A real message is written only if someone actually joined.
  • Leaving is silent. No "Alp left" toast inside the huddle. The avatars just change.
  • Alone for 3 minutes: the huddle ends itself, with no "nobody came" moment.

The bottom dock: one layout system for everything near the composer

What goes wrong today

  • The composer column in room_screen.dart stacks, bottom to top: field → media strip → stickers → scheduled pill → mention picker → reply card. With a scheduled message pending, the pill wedges between the reply card and the field it belongs to. That's the reply + scheduled bug.
  • The sync pill (center) and the scroll buttons (right) live in a separate floating stack with its own offset. Nothing stops a new center item (a Join pill, a toast) from landing on the sync pill.

Motion: Telegram and Duolingo energy

Our current chat-chrome rules (docs/design/chat-chrome-language.md) are deliberately quiet: small "kicks" that go out and home, no colour flashes, no loops. That's right for buttons you tap a hundred times a day. But you want impact, so I propose two layers:

Chrome: stays crisp

Buttons, pills, the dock, headers. Fast spring presses and glyph kicks, as today. Things you touch constantly shouldn't perform every time.

Moments: go big

Rare, meaningful events get full motion graphics: particles, squash and stretch, overshoot, colour waves, a beat of celebration, always synced with haptics.

Guardrails that don't move: 60fps on the low-end Android in profile mode; never animate a blur; Reduce Motion and low power get short crossfades; moment animations never block input (you can keep typing while they play); one moment at a time.

Haptics: a continuous, two-way ramp

As in Instagram, the vibration is part of the gesture itself, not a few separate clicks. It's a stream of ticks laid along the pull: sparse and soft at the start, closer and firmer toward the line. Pull back and it fades the same way: each detent you cross on the way down plays at its own (lower) strength, so retreating feels like the tension letting go. The sideways slide gets its own ramp toward each option. In the prototype, the meter under the readout shows every tick (and Android Chrome actually vibrates).

The ramp

PhaseTicks
Pull 0 → 96pt12 detents at ((i+1)/13)^0.6 of the way: gaps shrink from ~14pt to ~5pt, strength 0.15 → 0.75. Crossed in either direction; strength follows position, not direction.
Fork lineOne distinct notch (rigid, 0.9). It feels like a click into place.
Slide 0 → 40pt6 detents toward the side you're moving, 0.2 → 0.7. Crossing the middle resets them, so switching sides ramps the new side from zero.
ArmedFirm tick (0.85). Disarming plays a soft one (0.3).
Commit / cancelCommit: medium impact. Cancel: a single soft tick. Silence after both.

At most one tick per frame: if a fast flick crosses three detents in one frame, it plays the strongest once. The ramp runs off the same value that draws the ring, so the two can never get out of step, and letting go stops it instantly.

How it's played

  • Not plain HapticFeedback. That has only 4 fixed strengths, which is why HapticRamp steps selection → light → medium. A smooth ramp needs intensity, so we add tick(intensity) to the existing monkeyhub/haptics channel.
  • iOS: UIImpactFeedbackGenerator(.light).impactOccurred(intensity:), .prepare()d on touch-down so the first tick has no lag. The notch uses .rigid.
  • Android 11+ with primitives: VibrationEffect.Composition PRIMITIVE_LOW_TICK / TICK with a scale, the crisp Pixel/Samsung-flagship feel.
  • Android with amplitude control: 8ms one-shots with amplitude = intensity.
  • Generalize, don't fork: HapticRamp gains a two-way mode and an intensity callback; the send button's hold ring keeps working as it does today.

Guards

ConditionWhat changesWhy
Low power (LowPowerMode.active: iOS Low Power, Battery Saver, or ≤20% and not charging)Landmarks only: fork notch, arm, commit. No continuous ramp.The app already goes calm in these modes (motion off). The vibration motor is one of the hungrier parts of a phone, and the user asked the phone to save power. Landmarks keep the gesture readable.
Cheap vibration motor (Android without hasAmplitudeControl())Landmarks only.These motors can't do short, light ticks; a fast ramp turns into a mushy buzz that feels broken. Better 3 clean bumps than a rattle.
Amplitude but no primitivesRamp thinned to 6 detents, ≥40ms apart.One-shots below ~40ms apart blur on mid-range motors.
User turned off touch feedback (Android HAPTIC_FEEDBACK_ENABLED = 0)Silent.Calling Vibrator directly bypasses that setting (performHapticFeedback respects it, raw vibrations don't), so we check it ourselves. iOS "System Haptics" off already silences the generator.
In a call (only vanish is offered then)Landmarks only.On many Androids the motor's buzz leaks into the microphone.
Janky framesNothing extra: one tick per frame, driven by progress.A slow phone gets fewer ticks, never a burst of catch-up ticks.
User's own choiceAn explicit "Full motion" setting does not force the ramp under low power.Motion and power are different promises; haptics follow the power one.

The tier is decided once per gesture at touch-down (one cached native query for the motor's capabilities at launch), so nothing is re-checked per tick.

Edge cases

CaseBehaviour
Keyboard up, context card open, recording, selection mode, mention picker openGesture off.
A new message arrives mid-pullThe list is pinned during the gesture; the message inserts after release.
Vanish turned on by someone else while you're mid-gestureOptions are captured at touch-down. Committing "Turn on" when it's already on is a no-op with a toast ("Already on"), never a second system line.
Commit fails (offline, call full)The orb shakes once, the reason shows, then it collapses.
Someone turns vanish off while a 10s ring is drainingMessages already counting down still vanish (the promise was made when they were sent). New messages stay.
VoiceOver / TalkBackThe composer exposes "Start huddle" and "Turn on/off vanish mode" as custom actions.
DiscoverabilityThe first 3 times you land at the bottom of a chat, a one-second chevron peeks above the composer. After you've used the gesture, never again.

Build and release: one update, everywhere

Agreed: it ships as one release on every platform, not in pieces. The build order inside that is about risk, not release: each step is built on the one before it and stays on dev until the whole set is done.

Release

  1. Backend first, purely additive (new optional fields, new functions). Old builds keep working in normal rooms.
  2. Raise the minimum supported version with this release. An old build in a vanish room wouldn't block screenshots or hide previews, which breaks the promise. The store-driven update row already supports a forced minimum, and this matches the plan to make the next release a forced minimum anyway. Backend and app go out the same day, so the gap where an old build could sit in a vanish room is the hours before the forced update lands.
  3. All platforms together: iOS, Android, macOS, Windows, web, Linux. Web and Linux get sealed bubbles and the room-info toggle, but no gesture.
  4. Before you upload: on-device pass in profile mode on a high-end and a low-end Android plus an iPhone (haptic tiers, dock collisions, burn timing), then the VoIP-style two-phone test for huddles.

Tests: controller thresholds and hysteresis; floor math (slowest reader, hider excluded, late joiner, leaver, old client); burn idempotency; 24h chain; huddle never writes a ring signal; 200-member push fan-out; goldens for the dock collision rows, the room sheet in every theme, and the fork states.
